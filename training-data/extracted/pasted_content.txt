### 1. Core idea: what your “Modeled Hair Engine” actually does

Think in terms of **signals**, not magic.

Your engine should take a photo and output a small, opinionated set of tags:

- **Hair presence:** visible / not visible  
- **Hair length:** short / medium / long  
- **Curl pattern:** straight / wavy / curly / coily (optionally 1–4C later)  
- **Texture & density:** fine / medium / thick (approximate, not medical)  
- **Hair color:** black / brown / blonde / red / gray / fantasy  
- **Protective / styled state:** natural / blown out / braided / locs / wig (later phase)  

You don’t need everything day one. Start with:  
**length + curl pattern + basic color** — and make it work beautifully.

---

### 2. Use Google + Mediapipe as your “sensors”

You already chose Google Vision. Add Mediapipe’s Image Segmenter / Hair Segmentation as your second sensor.

- **Google Vision:**
  - **Face detection + landmarks** → head pose, face area, hairline region  
  - **Image properties** → dominant colors in hair region  
  - **Labels** → “long hair,” “blond hair,” “black hair,” etc. (weak but usable hints)

- **Mediapipe Image Segmenter / Hair Segmentation:**
  - Dedicated model for **person’s hair only** segmentation  
  - Gives you a **hair mask**: every pixel labeled as hair vs not hair  
  - Runs on image, video, or live stream, with outputs as category/confidence masks  
  - Legacy “Hair Segmentation” solution already exists with graphs and example apps

Combined, they give you:
- where the hair is (mask)  
- what colors are in the hair region  
- where the head/face are for context  

That’s enough to build your own features.

---

### 3. Phase 1: rule‑based system (no training yet)

Start scrappy. You can get **surprisingly far** with segmentation + simple heuristics.

#### A. Hair length (short / medium / long)

- **Step 1:** Use Mediapipe to get the **hair mask**.  
- **Step 2:** Compare hair mask height vs face bounding box height (from Google Vision).
  - **Short:** hair pixels mostly above jawline  
  - **Medium:** hair reaches roughly shoulders  
  - **Long:** hair extends significantly below shoulders / face box

You’re not measuring in centimeters; you’re inferring from relative proportions.

#### B. Hair color (basic categories)

- **Step 1:** Use hair mask to isolate hair region pixels.  
- **Step 2:** Compute dominant color(s) in that region (mean hue / histogram).  
- **Step 3:** Map color ranges to:
  - black  
  - dark brown  
  - light brown  
  - blonde  
  - red/auburn  
  - gray/white  
  - fantasy (blue/green/pink)

#### C. Curl pattern (straight / wavy / curly / coily – version 1)

You can approximate without ML:

- **Step 1:** Within the hair mask, compute:
  - **edge density** (how many edges per area – curls = more edges)  
  - **texture roughness** (local variance)  
- **Step 2:** Heuristic thresholds:
  - low edges, low variance → straight  
  - medium edges, medium variance → wavy  
  - high edges, high variance, visible loops → curly  
  - very high with tight clusters around head → coily

This won’t be 1–4C‑perfect, but it will give you a **first‑pass curl flag** you can improve later.

### 4. Phase 2: train Modeled’s own classifier

Once you have your rule‑based system, use it as scaffolding for a real model.

#### A. Define your label space

Keep it simple first:

- **Curl type:** straight / wavy / curly / coily  
- **Length:** short / medium / long  

Optionally later:

- **Curl pattern detail:** 2A–2C, 3A–3C, 4A–4C  
- **Density:** fine / medium / thick  

#### B. Build a labeled dataset

- **Collect:**  
  - use your own model photos (with consent)  
  - public hair datasets if available  
  - synthetic / scraped with care (respect licenses)

- **Label:**  
  - Have a small group of humans label:
    - curl type  
    - length  
    - approximate density  
  - Use a simple web UI: image + dropdowns.

You don’t need millions of images. A few **thousand well‑labeled** images can already work with transfer learning.

#### C. Train a lightweight model

- **Base:** use a pretrained CNN/ViT (e.g., MobileNet, EfficientNet)  
- **Input:** cropped hair region (using Mediapipe mask)  
- **Output heads:**
  - curl type classifier  
  - length classifier  
  - optional density classifier

You’re essentially teaching the model: “when hair looks like this segment, it belongs to this bucket.”

#### D. Calibrate with your community

You can **ask your Models to confirm**:

- “We think your hair is: curly, medium, dark brown. Does this feel right?”  
- Let them adjust:
  - “Actually I’m wavy”  
  - “This is my stretched/blowout texture, my natural is 4C”

This does two things:
- improves accuracy over time (feedback → retrain)  
- feels aligned with Modeled’s ethos (self‑definition)

---

### 5. Phase 3: bias + inclusivity work (non‑negotiable)

You care about this deeply, so bake it in:

- **Diverse training set:**  
  - all curl patterns  
  - all skin tones  
  - protective styles (braids, locs, wigs, weaves)  
- **Separate tag for state:**  
  - “natural texture visible” vs “hair in protective style / wig / silk press”  
- **Don’t erase protective styles:**  
  - don’t force a curl type when the natural hair is not visible  
  - tag as “protective style: braids/locs/etc.” instead  

Run regular checks:
- **By skin tone:** is accuracy worse for darker skin tones?  
- **By style:** does it mislabel braids as “coily” or “frizzy”?

Where it struggles, let the human override be canon.
